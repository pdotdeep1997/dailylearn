Humanity's First Killer App Was a Spreadsheet

The earliest writing isn't poetry or scripture. It's receipts — lists of grain and sheep. And that boring origin explains everything.

Builds on↩ The Worst Good Idea in History

We picture writing beginning with an epic poem or a sacred law. Nope. The oldest writing on Earth is basically accounting — tallies of grain, sheep, and taxes owed. Disappointing? Only until you see why: once farming made surplus (yesterday’s lesson), somebody had to track who stored what and who owed whom. Writing was invented to handle information that outgrew human memory. It was civilization’s first killer app — and every tech leap since, right up to the internet, rhymes with it.

From clay tokens to cuneiform

In ancient Mesopotamia, traders first tracked goods with little clay tokens — one shape per commodity. As trade grew, they pressed the tokens’ marks into clay tablets, then simplified those marks into symbols. By ~3200 BCE this became cuneiform — wedge-shaped signs in clay. Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica each cooked up their own scripts, too. Independent invention again — same pattern as farming.

Crucially, early writing recorded quantities and things, not speech. Only later did symbols come to stand for sounds — and then writing could capture anything language could: names, laws, stories, ideas.

💡
Why it broke history open

Writing let information escape a single skull. It could be stored longer than memory, copied and carried across distance, and accumulated across generations. Knowledge stopped dying with the knower. Every information technology since — the printing press, the telegraph, the computer, the internet — does those same three jobs (store, transmit, accumulate) at ever-bigger scale.

📜
And it minted power

Whoever could write could run things — levy taxes, enforce contracts, issue laws, manage a bureaucracy bigger than any chief could hold in his head. Writing and the state grew up together, which is why literacy was long hoarded by a small scribe class. Control of information has meant power since the very first tablet.

The gist

  • The earliest writing was accounting, not literature — grain, sheep, taxes.

  • It arose to manage surplus-driven information beyond human memory (builds on the farming lesson).

  • It evolved tokens → marks → cuneiform, and was independently invented in several places.

  • Only later did symbols encode sounds, unlocking writing anything language can say.

  • Writing lets knowledge be stored, copied, accumulated — and hands power to whoever controls it.
