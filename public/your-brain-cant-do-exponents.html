Why Your Brain Can't Feel Exponential Growth

Fold a paper 42 times and it reaches the Moon. Your gut says 'a few inches.' That gap explains pandemics, compound interest, and viral everything.

Builds on↩ Spot the Bad Argument

A sheet of paper is ~0.1mm thick. Fold it in half 42 times and it would reach the Moon. Your gut is screaming that’s impossible — and that scream is the single most expensive blind spot in human reasoning. We think in straight lines; reality often moves in curves that explode. Pandemics, compound interest, viral growth, climate — all run on exponentials our intuition simply cannot feel. Today: how to override the blind spot.

Linear brain, exponential world

Our intuition is linear: add the same amount each step (1, 2, 3, 4…). Exponentials multiply each step (1, 2, 4, 8, 16…). For a while they look similar — which lulls you — then the exponential detonates. Thirty linear steps of +1 gets you to 30. Thirty exponential steps of ×2 gets you to over a billion. Same number of steps, unimaginably different destinations.

💡
The tricks to override it

You can’t feel exponentials, so use tools. Doubling time: ask ‘how long to double?’ and count doublings — each one matches everything that came before it combined. Rule of 72: 72 ÷ growth-rate ≈ years to double (from the compounding lesson!). Watch the rate, not the level: in exponential processes, today’s small number plus a steady growth rate tells you tomorrow’s scary one.

⚠️
It cuts both ways

Exponential growth builds fortunes (compounding) and crises (runaway debt, unchecked spread). Exponential decay is just as sneaky — skills, machines, and advantages erode faster than they feel like they should. The lesson isn’t ‘exponentials are good’ — it’s ‘when something compounds, your gut will be wrong, so reach for the math.’

The gist

  • Intuition is linear (add); many real processes are exponential (multiply).

  • They look similar early, then the exponential explodes — that’s the trap.

  • 30 steps of +1 → 30; 30 steps of ×2 → over a billion.

  • Override with doubling time, the Rule of 72, and watching the growth rate.

  • It cuts both ways — growth and decay compound faster than they feel.
