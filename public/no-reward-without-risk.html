There's No Free Lunch: Risk and Return

Anyone promising high returns with no risk is lying or confused. Understand the trade-off and you'll never fall for it.

Builds on↩ Putting a Price on the Future

If an investment offered guaranteed 20% a year with zero risk, everyone would pour money in until the return got competed down to boring. That never-quite-happens is the deepest law of markets: you only get higher expected returns by taking more risk. No exceptions that last. Today: what ‘risk’ actually means, and why the promise of high-return-no-risk is always a red flag.

Risk = how much it can swing

In finance, ‘risk’ mostly means volatility — how much an investment’s value bounces around. A savings account barely moves (low risk, low return). Stocks lurch up and down (higher risk) and, on average over long periods, pay more to compensate you for the stomach-churning ride.

Why the link? Because investors dislike uncertainty. To get people to hold a bumpy asset, it has to dangle a higher expected reward — the risk premium. Remove the premium and nobody holds the risky thing.

⚠️
The scam detector

“High returns, guaranteed, no risk” is the signature of a fraud (see: every Ponzi scheme). Real high returns come with real chances of loss. If someone has genuinely found risk-free excess return, they don’t need your money — and the market would erase it fast anyway.

💡
Risk ≠ ruin, though

Volatility isn’t the only risk. The one that actually matters is permanent loss — being forced to sell at the bottom, or an asset going to zero. A bumpy asset you can hold for 20 years may be less risky for you than a ‘stable’ one that quietly erodes to inflation. Match the risk to your time horizon and your ability to not panic.

The gist

  • Higher expected return requires taking more risk — the risk premium.

  • ‘Risk’ usually means volatility (how much value swings), but the real danger is permanent loss.

  • Cash < bonds < stocks < speculation, roughly, on the risk–return line.

  • ‘High return, no risk, guaranteed’ = a red flag for fraud, every time.

  • The right risk level depends on your time horizon and your nerve.
