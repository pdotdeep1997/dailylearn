The Most Important Curve in Finance (It Prints Money While You Sleep)

Would you take $100 now or $100 next year? Pin down *why* you said 'now' and you've unlocked how to value almost anything.

Builds on↩ Money Is a Story We All Believe

$100 today or $100 in a year? You said now — obviously. But why? Nail the reason and you’ve cracked the single most important idea in all of finance: the time value of money. It’s the engine under loans, mortgages, investments, and that scary yield curve we’ll meet later. Yesterday we said money is a store of value; today we discover the store quietly leaks — and that leak is measurable, exploitable, and kind of magical.

Why later money is worth less

A dollar today beats a dollar tomorrow for three stacked reasons:

  • Opportunity — invest today’s dollar and it grows.
  • Risk — a promised future dollar might never show up.
  • Inflation — a future dollar probably buys less.

So future money has to be discounted to compare it with money now. The rate we discount at — the interest rate — is basically the price of time.

Compounding: interest on your interest

Put money to work at some interest rate and it grows. The magic: next year you earn interest on last year’s interest too. That’s compounding, and it curves upward instead of climbing in a straight line.

$100 at 10%/yr → $110, then $121 (not $120 — you earned interest on the extra $10), then $133… After 30 years: about $1,745. From a boring 10%. The formula is Future = Present × (1 + r)ⁿ, and all the drama lives in that little exponent n.

💡
Run it backwards = value ANYTHING

Here’s the payoff. If money grows forward by ×(1+r), then to find what future money is worth today, you run it backwards — you divide: Present = Future / (1 + r)ⁿ. That’s discounting. $110 next year at 10% is worth $100 now. This one move lets you price a bond, a stock, a rental, a whole company — translate any future cash into today’s dollars and compare. Almost all of valuation is just this.

The gist

  • Money has a time value: now beats later (opportunity, risk, inflation).

  • The interest rate is the price of time.

  • Compounding earns interest on interest: Future = Present × (1+r)ⁿ — a curve, not a line.

  • Discounting reverses it (Present = Future / (1+r)ⁿ) — the key to valuing any future cash.

  • We underrate compounding because we think in straight lines — so fees and small rates matter hugely over time.
