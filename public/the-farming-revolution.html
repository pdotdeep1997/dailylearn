The Worst Good Idea in Human History

Planting seeds made us sicker, shorter, and more overworked than our ancestors. It also built literally everything. Both things are true.

For ~200,000 years humans were foragers — roaming, grabbing what nature offered, working shockingly few hours. Then, about 12,000 years ago, some genius planted seeds and waited. And here’s the twist historians love: for the individual human, it was arguably a terrible deal — worse diet, backbreaking labour, new diseases. Yet it produced cities, kings, writing, money, and you reading this. Farming is the deal humanity couldn’t refuse. First lesson of the History track.

The one word that changed everything: surplus

Foragers ate what they found. Farmers produced food — and, crucially, produced more than they needed right now. That leftover is the hinge of all history: surplus.

Surplus meant not everyone had to chase dinner. Suddenly some people could do something else full-time — make pots, forge weapons, pray, rule, keep records. That’s specialization, and it’s the engine behind every complex society that followed. No surplus, no specialists, no civilization.

⚠️
The catch nobody mentions

Early farmers were often worse off than foragers: longer hours, a dull grain-heavy diet, shorter stature, and shiny new diseases from crowding together with animals. Stored surplus could also be stolen — hello taxes, warfare, and the first real inequality. Farming won not because it made people happier, but because it made more people and more power. Quantity beat quality.

📜
It happened 7+ times, alone

Farming was invented independently — Fertile Crescent, China, Mesoamerica, the Andes, and more — by peoples who never met. When that many separate groups stumble on the same trick, it’s a clue that the conditions (a stable, warming climate after the Ice Age) were ripe. History is driven by circumstance, not just heroes.

The gist

  • ~12,000 years ago humans went from foraging to farming — producing food, not just finding it.

  • The real product was surplus: more food than needed right now.

  • Surplus freed people to specialize → cities → states, taxes, writing.

  • For individuals it was often a downgrade (disease, toil, inequality) — but it made more people and power.

  • Invented independently in many places → conditions, not lone geniuses, drive history.
